# Contract-Sanity Checklist — how to use it

The one-sentence version: before signing, read it once for money, once for
rights, once for exits — then check the ten boxes.

## Two ways to use it

**Blank:** `python3 build.py`, print `printables/contract-sanity-checklist.pdf`,
keep a few by the desk for surprise opportunities.

**On screen:** open `forms/contract-checklist.html` in a browser, jot notes in
the boxes, **Ctrl+P / Cmd+P → Save as PDF**.

## How to run it on a real contract

1. **Read the contract** before any checklist. Sounds obvious; almost nobody does.
   One slow read, highlights allowed.
2. **Check the ten boxes.** Each box points at a clause type that quietly hurts.
   On a clean contract, most boxes tick fast.
3. **Anything that didn't tick:** write it as a plain question in the
   *open questions* box. "When exactly does invoice payment become due?" is a
   perfectly normal email to send. Contracts are negotiable more often than
   people assume — especially for first-time freelancers the other side wants
   to keep.
4. **Decide and date it.** Three honest outcomes: sign, ask-questions-first, or
   walk. All three are wins compared to failing to look.

## The traps the boxes point at (plain English)

- **"Net 60" or "on approval"** — payment timing fog. Ask for a date.
- **Work-for-hire / assignment language** — you can lose ownership of your own
  work. Fine when priced for it, bad when surprised by it.
- **Kill fees and cancellation-after-delivery** — the two spots where
  "cancelled" suddenly means "unpaid".
- **Exclusivity / non-compete** — easy to nod at today, strangling in month six.
- **Governing law & venue** — where a person who is not either of you decides
  everything, boringly, expensively.

## Not legal advice, on purpose

This is a *thinking aid* built from common freelance flaws, made so you never
sign on autopilot. It doesn't substitute for a lawyer. For big money, confusing
terms, or anything with the words "indemnify", a short consult with a licensed
professional in your state is the right spend.
