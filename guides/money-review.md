# Weekly Money Review — how to use it

The one-sentence version: same day and time every week, twenty minutes, pen first.

## Two ways to use it

**Blank:** `python3 build.py`, print a small stack of
`printables/weekly-money-review.pdf`. One sheet per week.

**On screen:** open `forms/money-review.html` in a browser, tick boxes (the text
boxes accept typing), then **Ctrl+P / Cmd+P → Save as PDF**.

## Set the ritual before the checklist

Decide once: *which day, what time, where.* Sunday morning with coffee and a
closed laptop is a good default; any consistent slot works. Put the next five
reviews in your calendar right now — "deciding **when** later" is how the ritual
dies. This page pairs well with the Expense Log: review day is when the log gets
its weekly total.

## The checklist, grouped by what it actually does

- **Inputs** — receipts gathered, expenses logged. This is just closing the loop
  the week created, while the week is still fresh.
- **Outputs (money coming to you)** — invoices sent, payments marked, overdue
  nudges sent. The nudge email exists in the customer email templates sheet; use
  it without guilt, it's what normal businesses do.
- **Awareness** — bank balance snapshot, set-aside tax slice, next week's
  deadlines, one number to watch. None of this is a grade; it's a dashboard.

## "One number I'm watching"

Pick exactly one per week: this week's spent, or unpaid invoices, or the bank
balance trend. Writing *one line on why* is what turns a number into a nudge —
"watching unpaid invoices: 3 sit past due, including BigCo" beats a vague dread.

## The fine print, emotionally

Miss a week? The only rule that matters is: **just do the next one.** This is a
feedback loop, not a report card. Streaks are pleasant, recovery is the skill.
