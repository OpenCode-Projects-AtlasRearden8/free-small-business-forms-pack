# Expense Log — how to use it

The one-sentence version: one line per purchase, same day, receipts in the same order.

## Two ways to use it

**Blank:** `python3 build.py`, then print as many copies of
`printables/expense-log.pdf` as there are months. A clipboard version beats a
software version you never open.

**On screen:** open `forms/expense-log.html` in a browser, type, **Ctrl+P /
Cmd+P → Save as PDF** (or just print on paper).

## The columns, decoded

- **Date** — the day you *paid*, not the day you remembered.
- **What it was for** — "Domain renewal (dylanasdale.com)", not "misc". Future
  you, and possibly a tax preparer, both read this.
- **Category** — keep a short personal list and reuse it ruthlessly. A good
  starter set for a book-and-tools business:
  *software & subscriptions · printing & shipping · advertising · hosting &
  domains · education/books · equipment · fees (payment platform) · office
  supplies · travel*. Six or seven categories you actually use beat fifteen you
  meant to use.
- **Rec.** — tick this when you can *find* the receipt again. Scanned photo on
  your phone counts. An unticked box is a small to-do, not a shame flag.
- **Amount** — number only; `12.49`, no symbols.

## Weekly two-minute close

Add the column, write the total next to **Total spent this period**, and tally
the categories into the totals box. That's it — the sheet is done until next week.

## Why bother (the honest version)

Tax time is just this sheet, twelve times, plus patience. That's the whole product.

## When you outgrow it

When one page stops fitting a busy month: it's a good sign, and it's software
time (spreadsheets carry most small businesses for years). Keep a few finished
PDF or paper sheets anyway — they make a tidy, printable paper trail.
