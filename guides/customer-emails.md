# Customer Email Templates — how to use it

The one-sentence version: copy one, swap the blanks, press send. Warm beats wordy.

## Two ways to use it

**Blank:** `python3 build.py`, print `printables/customer-email-templates.pdf`
and keep it next to the desk. The **[underlined slots]** are write-in blanks.

**On screen (recommended):** open `forms/customer-emails.html` in a browser,
type right into the slots, then copy each finished email into your mail app —
or Ctrl+P / Cmd+P → Save as PDF to keep filled-in versions on file.

## The six, and when each earns its keep

1. **The quote / first contact** — someone said "interested." Ask *one*
   question, promise a date, stop. Speed here quietly gains jobs.
2. **The follow-up that isn't annoying** — one nudge, an explicit exit
   ("just say the word"), no expiry-date pressure theater. People reply to
   breathing room.
3. **The invoice email** — attach the PDF, state the amount and due date in the
   body. Email subject lines like "Invoice #2026-004 — due Mar 15" get paid first.
4. **The friendly late-payment nudge** — assumes a crossed wire, not a villain.
   One polite email recovers more invoices than 25 belongs-to-a-collections-agent
   walls ever will.
5. **Delivering the work** — end with the limits of revisions, stated kindly,
   stated *before* it matters.
6. **The thank-you** — reviews and referrals asked on the same warm day, once,
   lightly. Small businesses run on this email.

## Three small rules for all of them

- **Blank-swap discipline:** fill every underlined slot. A leftover `NAME` is a
  worse look than a typo. Re-read once.
- **Sign-off:** your actual name every time. It's the whole brand at this stage.
- **Keep your best versions:** when a variant converts well, save it as a mail
  draft or template. This sheet is the starting library, not the law.

## Note on tone

Every template is deliberately human: short paragraphs, no jargon, one clear
ask. That's the house style — explaining things "like a friend would" applies
to your customers too.
