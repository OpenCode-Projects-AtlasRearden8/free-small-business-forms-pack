# Content Calendar — how to use it

The one-sentence version: fifteen minutes on Sunday, one week of ideas, then live your week.

## Two ways to use it

**Blank:** `python3 build.py`, print `printables/content-calendar.pdf`,
put it where coffee happens.

**On screen:** open `forms/content-calendar.html` in a browser, type into the
grid, **Ctrl+P / Cmd+P → Save as PDF**.

## The grid, in honest terms

- **Channel** — one per row is the whole trick. "Newsletter" is a plan;
  "newsletter, Instagram, YouTube, blog post, and a LinkedIn thing" is a Sunday
  anxiety attack. Empty rows are fine.
- **Idea in one line** — half-baked is the correct level of done for this page.
  "Post the chapter-zero checklist" is a finished row. If you catch yourself
  writing the actual content here, you're a day early, not behind.

## The idea parking lot

Every creator brain produces more ideas than schedule. That's what the parking
lot is for: **write it down, don't start it.** Next week, the calendar borrows
from the lot before your brain has to. A fed parking lot makes "I have nothing
to post" permanently untrue.

## A 15-minute ritual

1. Look at last week's sheet (or skip, if the week got away — no guilt ledger here).
2. Pick **one** theme for the week, fill in the top line.
3. Give each planned day a channel and a one-liner. Count to three first;
   three real posts beat seven imaginary ones.
4. Park every extra idea. Close the page. Go do the week.

## Done column

Only two states: ticked or not. A post is "done" when it's *live*, not polished.
Unticked rows roll into the parking lot with zero commentary.
