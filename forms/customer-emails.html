Tip: keep it short and kind. Send, then stop refreshing. See guides/customer-emails.md.

Customer Email Templates

Copy one, swap the blanks, press send. Warm beats wordy.

1. The quote / first contact

Subject:

Hi First name,

Thanks for reaching out about project/need, in their words. I’d love to help.

Quick question so I can quote it accurately: one thing only. Once I have that, I’ll send options by day.

— Your name

2. The follow-up that isn’t annoying

Subject:

Hi First name,

Just floating this back to the top of your inbox — no rush. The quote for project still stands ($X, timeframe), and the offer doesn’t expire.

If it’s a “not now,” just say the word and I’ll stop nudging.

— Your name

3. The invoice (with the polite deadline)

Subject:

Hi First name,

All done — one line on what was delivered. Invoice #number for $X is attached, due date.

You can pay by methods. Questions welcome, anytime.

— Your name

4. The friendly late-payment nudge

Subject:

Hi First name,

Gentle bookkeeping nudge: invoice #number ($X) was due date and I don’t see it on my end. It may just be a crossed wire.

Could you pop it over this week, or let me know if there’s an issue with it? Happy to resend anything.

— Your name

5. Delivering the work

Subject:

Hi First name,

Here’s deliverable: link or “attached”.

Two things to help you use it: tip 1, and tip 2. Tweak requests are included through date/rounds.

— Your name

6. The thank-you (that quietly invites the next job)

Subject:

Hi First name,

Thanks for working with me on project — I enjoyed it.

If it went well, a short review or a one-line testimonial would genuinely help my small business: link. And if anyone you know needs service, I’m warm to intros.

— Your name

Save your favorite versions as drafts. Your best email is the one you actually send.
